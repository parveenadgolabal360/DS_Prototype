How a counter-move is decided

The agent never answers a price move on price alone. It checks the pair's corridor, whether the rival is in our tier, our margin after the counter, the day's trade budget, and whether the brand is on the premium lock list. Inside every guardrail it executes and tells the account manager; outside any one of them it drafts the move and waits for you.

auto-execute ⇔ depth ≤ max auto depth ∧ margin after ≥ floor ∧ spend ≤ daily cap left ∧ ¬(premium brand ∧ mass-tier rival) channel = platform API where one exists (Amazon) · account manager email (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart) verify = the next price sweep on the same pin codes reads the shelf price back
Write-back is mocked in this prototype. Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart expose no price API, so on those platforms "execute" means an email to the account manager plus a verification sweep. Amazon moves use the Amazon Ads and Coupons API.

Unilever's weekly spend, back-calculated

We cannot see their ad console. We can see which sponsored slots they hold, at which position, four times a day, and we know what those slots cost us. The rest is arithmetic.

₹ lakh per week · range is the ±22% band
How the spend is estimated
HUL ₹/week (keyword) ≈ weekly searches × HUL paid impression share × CTR at HUL's position × est. CPC HUL ₹/week (browse) ≈ category-page sponsored impressions held by HUL × est. CPM

Searches come from the platform's search-volume index, allocated to NCR by category off-take share. Paid impression share and position come from our own crawl of sponsored slots, four sweeps a day per pin-code cluster. CTR by position is fitted on our own campaigns in the same category and platform. CPC is what we paid for the same slot at the same hour, ±18%.

Read it as a range. The errors compound: the ±22% band is the propagated uncertainty, and it is wider for keywords where we do not bid ourselves, because there we have no clearing price of our own to anchor the CPC.

Blinkit campaign structure — can we read spend by city?

Blinkit reports media at campaign level, and campaigns are built per city. A campaign that spans several cities returns one number for all of them, so city-level return cannot be attributed. The fix is structural: one campaign per city.

—
Budgets for the split campaigns are proposed on each city's share of category off-take, so the total weekly spend does not change on day one. Nothing else about the campaigns changes: keywords, bids and creatives are copied as they are.

Losses from replenishing at the minimum

When a consignment is sized at the bottom of Blinkit's Min–Max band, or a platform suppresses a PO below MOQ, the store covers the morning and runs out in the evening peak. This is what that cost last week, and when the stock needs to land instead.

How a lost point gets exactly one lever

Deterministic, not modelled. Each rung repairs one condition and measures only what the rungs above it could not explain, so no share point is counted twice.
